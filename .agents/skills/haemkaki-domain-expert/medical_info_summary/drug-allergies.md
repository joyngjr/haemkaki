# Drug Allergies and Hypersensitivities: Commonly Implicated Medicines

> **Status:** Web-researched clinical reference, 18 September 2026  
> **Issuing skill:** `firecrawl`  
> **Intended use:** HaemKaki drug-allergy search terms and clinical-domain reference  
> **Not intended as:** A diagnosis, a prediction that a patient is allergic, or an automatic drug-exclusion rule

## Scope and safety boundary

There is no medically valid finite list of **all** drugs that can cause allergy: virtually any medicine can cause an immune-mediated reaction in a susceptible person. The list below contains drugs and drug classes that are **frequently reported, commonly evaluated, or particularly important causes of drug hypersensitivity**. It is intentionally broader than confirmed IgE-mediated allergy because patient records often use “allergy” for several clinically important hypersensitivity phenotypes.

An allergy entry should store the **specific active ingredient**, reaction phenotype, timing, severity, date/age at reaction, route, certainty (`reported`, `suspected`, `confirmed`, or `disproved`), and any tolerated related drugs. A class-wide allergy must not be inferred automatically from a reaction to one ingredient.

Penicillin allergy is especially over-reported. CDC reports that approximately 10% of the US population reports it, while objective evaluation usually confirms allergy in only a small minority. Appropriate specialist evaluation and delabelling should be supported rather than treating every historical label as permanent.

## 2. Analgesics and anti-inflammatory medicines

### Aspirin and non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs)

- Aspirin (acetylsalicylic acid)
- Ibuprofen
- Naproxen
- Diclofenac
- Indometacin (indomethacin)
- Ketorolac
- Ketoprofen
- Mefenamic acid
- Piroxicam
- Meloxicam
- Etoricoxib
- Celecoxib

> NSAID reactions include several distinct cross-reactive and selective phenotypes. Do not automatically infer that every patient reacting to one NSAID will react to all NSAIDs or to paracetamol/acetaminophen.

### Other analgesic hypersensitivity entries

- Paracetamol (acetaminophen)
- Opioid (class or unspecified)
- Morphine
- Codeine
- Oxycodone
- Hydromorphone
- Fentanyl
- Tramadol

> Opioids commonly produce non-allergic histamine-mediated symptoms. Record the exact reaction; itching or flushing alone should not automatically create a class-wide opioid-allergy rule.

## 3. Anticonvulsants associated with delayed hypersensitivity and SCAR

- Carbamazepine
- Oxcarbazepine
- Phenytoin
- Fosphenytoin
- Phenobarbital
- Primidone
- Lamotrigine
- Zonisamide

These medicines are important causes of severe cutaneous adverse reactions (SCAR), including DRESS, Stevens–Johnson syndrome (SJS), and toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN). SCAR histories must never be treated as candidates for routine rechallenge.

## 4. Gout and hyperuricaemia medicines

- Allopurinol
- Febuxostat

Allopurinol is a particularly important cause of morbilliform eruption, DRESS, SJS, and TEN.

## 5. Antiretroviral medicines

- Abacavir
- Nevirapine
- Efavirenz
- Etravirine
- Raltegravir

Abacavir hypersensitivity is strongly associated with HLA-B*57:01. A suspected or confirmed abacavir hypersensitivity reaction is a permanent contraindication to rechallenge.

## 6. Cancer chemotherapy

### Platinum compounds

- Carboplatin
- Cisplatin
- Oxaliplatin

### Taxanes

- Paclitaxel
- Docetaxel
- Cabazitaxel

### Other commonly evaluated antineoplastic agents

- Asparaginase
- Pegaspargase
- Methotrexate
- Doxorubicin
- Etoposide
- Irinotecan

Chemotherapy can cause IgE-mediated allergy, non-IgE hypersensitivity, cytokine-release reactions, and reactions to formulation excipients. Store the phenotype and formulation when known.

## 7. Monoclonal antibodies and other biologics

- Rituximab
- Cetuximab
- Infliximab
- Tocilizumab
- Omalizumab
- Trastuzumab
- Bevacizumab
- Adalimumab
- Etanercept

Infusion reactions are not automatically allergies. The precise biologic, infusion number, timing, symptoms, and any tolerated subsequent dose are important.

## 8. Peri-operative medicines

### Neuromuscular-blocking agents

- Rocuronium
- Suxamethonium (succinylcholine)
- Atracurium
- Cisatracurium
- Vecuronium
- Pancuronium
- Mivacurium

### Anaesthetic and peri-operative agents

- Propofol
- Thiopental
- Midazolam
- Sugammadex
- Protamine

### Local anaesthetics — allergy is reported but true allergy is uncommon

- Lidocaine (lignocaine)
- Bupivacaine
- Mepivacaine
- Prilocaine
- Ropivacaine
- Articaine
- Procaine
- Chloroprocaine
- Tetracaine
- Benzocaine

Peri-operative anaphylaxis may also be due to an antibiotic, chlorhexidine, latex, or another exposure rather than the anaesthetic itself. Specialist investigation should identify the culprit and safe alternatives.

## 9. Other clinically important drug-hypersensitivity entries

- Chlorhexidine
- Iodinated contrast medium (record the specific agent, e.g. iohexol or iopamidol)
- Gadolinium-based contrast medium (record the specific agent)
- Proton-pump inhibitors: omeprazole, esomeprazole, lansoprazole, pantoprazole, rabeprazole
- ACE inhibitors: captopril, enalapril, lisinopril, perindopril, ramipril
- Insulin (record the specific preparation)
- Heparin
- Low-molecular-weight heparins: enoxaparin, dalteparin, tinzaparin
- Clopidogrel
- Hydralazine
- Procainamide
- Propylthiouracil
- Bupropion
- Quinine

> ACE-inhibitor angioedema, heparin-induced thrombocytopenia, and many contrast reactions are not IgE-mediated allergy, but they are clinically important and require precise structured documentation. The app should not collapse mechanism-specific warnings into a generic “anaphylaxis risk” flag.

## 10. Excipients that may be the actual culprit

- Polyethylene glycol (PEG/macrogol)
- Polysorbate 80
- Gelatin
- Carboxymethylcellulose
- Dextran
- Cremophor EL (polyoxyethylated castor oil)
- Mannitol
- Povidone
- Benzyl alcohol
- Parabens
- Metabisulfite and other sulfites

Record an excipient separately from the drug’s active ingredient. Vaccine hypersensitivity is rare and may be due to an excipient rather than the vaccine antigen.

## Recommended data-model behavior

- Offer the individual generic ingredients above as searchable terms plus class/unspecified fallback terms.
- Preserve the patient’s original wording and brand name as aliases.
- Never expand one ingredient automatically into a class-wide allergy.
- Distinguish `allergy/hypersensitivity`, `intolerance/side effect`, and `unknown reaction`.
- Capture immediate reactions (for example urticaria, angioedema, bronchospasm, or anaphylaxis) separately from delayed reactions (for example morbilliform eruption, DRESS, SJS/TEN, AGEP, organ injury, or cytopenia).
- Treat SJS/TEN, DRESS, AGEP, haemolytic anaemia, hepatitis, nephritis, and other severe reactions as high-severity alerts even when they are not IgE-mediated.
- Support a `disproved/delabelled` state; do not delete the historical entry or silently reactivate it.
- Do not recommend challenge, testing, desensitisation, or an alternative drug without clinician/allergy-specialist review.
