# Haemophilia Medication List: Prophylaxis, On-Demand Treatment, and Common Adjuncts

> **Evidence review date:** 19 September 2026<br>
> **Scope:** Congenital haemophilia A and B (with or without inhibitors), acquired haemophilia A (AHA), and factor XI deficiency historically called “haemophilia C”.<br>
> **Purpose:** Clinical-domain reference for HaemKaki data modelling. This is not a prescribing protocol.

## How to read this list

- “Common dosing unit” describes how a prescription or administration is usually recorded; it is **not a recommended dose**.
- Product eligibility, age limits, dose, interval, breakthrough-bleed treatment, and laboratory monitoring are product-, patient-, and jurisdiction-specific. Store the actual haemophilia treatment centre (HTC) plan.
- The named products are the current US-licensed list from NBDF/MASAC Document 299, supplemented with internationally used generic therapies for AHA and factor XI deficiency. Brand availability differs by country.
- Factor activity is usually recorded in **international units (IU)**. Bypassing activity may be labelled **units (U)** or **micrograms (μg)**. These measures are not interchangeable.
- Routes: **IV** = intravenous; **SC** = subcutaneous; **PO** = oral; **IN** = intranasal.

### 1.4 Bypassing-agent prophylaxis for patients with inhibitors

Bypassing agents are used regularly in selected patients with FVIII or FIX inhibitors, although a licensed non-factor option may be preferred depending on haemophilia type and local guidance.

| Medication                                                                         | Common brands/examples | Common dosing unit    | Route |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------- | --------------------- | ----- |
| Activated prothrombin complex concentrate (aPCC; anti-inhibitor coagulant complex) | FEIBA                  | U/kg; total U         | IV    |
| Recombinant activated factor VII (rFVIIa), eptacog alfa (activated)                | NovoSeven RT           | μg/kg; total μg or mg | IV    |

Eptacog beta (activated; Sevenfact) is a bypassing agent used for episodic bleeding; it is not assumed to have the same routine-prophylaxis indication as eptacog alfa in every jurisdiction.

| Medication                                      | Typical role                                                                            | Common dosing unit    | Route                                   |

| Tranexamic acid or EACA                         | Adjunct for selected mucosal bleeds/procedures                                          | mg/kg/dose or g/dose  | PO or IV; topical use may be prescribed |

**Critical safety distinctions:**

- Emicizumab, concizumab, marstacimab, and fitusiran are prophylactic medicines; they do not stop an active bleed by themselves.
- Breakthrough treatment while using a non-factor agent must follow that product’s written plan. In particular, prolonged/high-dose aPCC exposure with emicizumab has caused thrombotic microangiopathy and thrombosis; rFVIIa is generally preferred when clinically appropriate.
- In haemophilia B with a FIX inhibitor and prior FIX allergy/anaphylaxis, FIX-containing products, including aPCC, may be unsafe.

### 2.4 Factor XI deficiency (“haemophilia C”)

Long-term routine prophylaxis is uncommon. Treatment is usually procedure- or bleed-specific and guided by personal bleeding history and procedure site, not the FXI activity alone.

| Medication or blood component                                                                  | Typical role                                                                                  | Common dosing unit                      | Route                                   |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Tranexamic acid                                                                                | Common first choice for dental and other high-fibrinolysis mucosal procedures/bleeding        | mg/kg/dose or g/dose                    | PO or IV; topical mouthwash may be used |
| EACA                                                                                           | Alternative antifibrinolytic                                                                  | mg/kg/dose or g/dose                    | PO or IV                                |
| Fresh frozen plasma (FFP), pathogen-reduced plasma, or solvent/detergent-treated pooled plasma | FXI replacement when significant bleeding risk warrants it and FXI concentrate is unavailable | mL/kg; total mL or number of units/bags | IV transfusion                          |
| Plasma-derived FXI concentrate                                                                 | Specialist perioperative replacement where available (not licensed in all countries)          | FXI IU/kg; total IU                     | IV                                      |
| rFVIIa, usually eptacog alfa (activated)                                                       | Rare specialist use, particularly with an FXI inhibitor; not routine                          | μg/kg                                   | IV                                      |

**Safety note:** FXI concentrate has clinically important thrombosis risk. Do not assume it can be combined with tranexamic acid or another antifibrinolytic; specialist guidance commonly avoids this combination.

Aspirin and non-selective NSAIDs such as ibuprofen, naproxen, and diclofenac can impair platelet function or increase gastrointestinal bleeding. They should not be presented in the app as routine haemophilia analgesics; use only under the patient’s clinician-directed plan.

## 5. Minimum structured fields for HaemKaki

For every prescribed product, store:

1. active ingredient and exact brand/product;
2. therapeutic role: regular prophylaxis, ITI, on-demand, perioperative, adjunct, AHA immunosuppression, or gene therapy;
3. haemophilia type and inhibitor status;
4. route, dose value, dose unit, concentration, and total administered amount;
5. prescribed interval or PRN indication, plus start/end dates;
6. infusion/injection timestamp and completion status;
7. product-specific breakthrough-bleed and missed-dose instructions;
8. batch/lot number and expiry for biologics where captured;
9. adverse reaction/allergy and response to treatment;
10. jurisdiction/label version and the supervising HTC.

Do not normalize **IU**, **U**, **μg**, **mg**, **mL**, and **vector genomes** into one generic dose unit. Do not let one product inherit another product’s dose, half-life, interval, age indication, or inhibitor indication.
